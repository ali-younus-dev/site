Planning a Kitchen for Pakistani Cooking: Layout, Ventilation and Appliances

F
Fotile Pakistan
November 5, 2026
9 min read

Most kitchen plans start with cabinets and colours. In a Pakistani home they should start with the cooking: high flames, a lot of oil, big pots, several dishes at once and a family that eats together. Plan for that first and the kitchen stays clean, cool and easy to work in for years.

01Start with how you cook

Before you draw anything, write down how your family actually uses the kitchen. How many burners are usually on at once? Do you fry daily, or a few times a week? Do you bake? Is there a cook as well as family members? How many people eat at a normal meal — and at Eid? These answers decide the hob size, the hood, the oven and how much counter space you need.

02Choose a layout that keeps the work zones close

The classic rule is a "work triangle" between the sink, the hob and the fridge — close enough to move quickly, far enough not to collide. In practice:

  • L-shaped: the most practical layout for most Pakistani homes, with the hob on one wall and the sink on the other.
  • Parallel (galley): efficient in narrow kitchens and apartments; keep at least about a metre between the two runs.
  • U-shaped: lots of counter space for big families and heavy cooking.
  • Island: beautiful in open-plan homes, but plan the island hood and ceiling ducting early.

Many larger homes keep a closed main kitchen for heavy cooking and a lighter show kitchen for breakfast and tea. If you do, put your strongest hood in the heavy-cooking kitchen.

03Place the hob and hood together

The hob and hood are one decision. Put the hob against an outside wall if you can, so the hood's duct can run outside in a short, straight line. Avoid placing the hob right next to a window or door: draughts push the flame around and blow smoke away from the hood. Choose a hood at least as wide as the hob. Our kitchen hood buying guide covers sizes and styles.

Plan the duct route before the carpenter starts. Moving a duct after the cabinets are made is expensive — and usually ends in a longer, weaker run.

04Gas, power and load-shedding

Decide early whether each appliance runs on gas or electricity, because the pipes and wiring go in before anything else.

  • Gas hobs: plan the gas line and an accessible shut-off valve. If you use LPG, keep the cylinder in a ventilated place and follow the installer's safety guidance.
  • Built-in ovens and induction hobs: they need suitable, dedicated power points. Ask for the exact requirement for your model before the electrician finishes.
  • Load-shedding: many homes keep a gas hob for everyday cooking and use electric ovens for baking. If you rely on a UPS or solar system, check it can support the appliances you want to run during outages.

05Built-in appliances need exact spaces

Built-in ovens, microwaves, dishwashers and refrigerators need precise cabinet openings and ventilation gaps. Choose the models first and give the cut-out sizes to your carpenter — not the other way round. A tall unit with the oven at eye level and a microwave above it saves bending and keeps hot trays away from children.

06Think about washing up

A family kitchen produces a mountain of dishes. A sink dishwasher fits where a normal sink goes and also washes fruit and vegetables, so it suits kitchens without space for a full-size dishwasher. Plan the water inlet, drain and power point under the sink.

07Surfaces that survive oil and heat

Choose a backsplash and counter that wipe clean easily — quartz, granite or large-format tiles with few grout lines. Glass-top hobs and smooth hood surfaces are faster to clean than ribbed or textured ones. Good lighting over the hob and prep area matters as much as the overhead light.

08Storage for big pots and bulk shopping

Deep drawers for degchis and karahis, a tall pantry for atta and rice, and a pull-out for spices close to the hob will make daily cooking much easier than open shelves.

09See it before you buy

Hoods and hobs look different in a showroom than in a catalogue. Visit a Fotile showroom or authorised dealer to see sizes and finishes, and ask the team to check your plan.
